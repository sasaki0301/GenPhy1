Motion in 2–3 Dimensions

Learning Objectives

  • Understand motion in multiple dimensions.
  • Resolve vectors into components.
  • Analyze projectile motion.
  • Describe horizontal and vertical motion independently.

1. Motion in More Than One Dimension

In real life, objects rarely move in a straight line. Instead, they move in two or three dimensions.

Key Idea: Motion in multiple dimensions can be analyzed by breaking it into independent components.

2. Vector Components

Any vector can be split into horizontal (x) and vertical (y) components.

vₓ = v cosθ
vᵧ = v sinθ

Vector broken into horizontal and vertical components

Example: A ball is thrown at 10 m/s at 30°.

vₓ = 10 cos30° ≈ 8.66 m/s
vᵧ = 10 sin30° = 5 m/s

3. Projectile Motion

Projectile motion occurs when an object moves under gravity alone after being launched.

  • Horizontal motion: constant velocity
  • Vertical motion: constant acceleration (gravity)

Parabolic motion of a projectile

4. Independence of Motion

Horizontal and vertical motions do not affect each other.

Key Idea: You can analyze x-motion and y-motion separately.

A ball is thrown horizontally.

  • Horizontal velocity stays constant.
  • Vertical velocity increases due to gravity.

5. Key Equations

Horizontal: x = vₓ t

Vertical: y = vᵧ t − ½gt²

vᵧ = vᵧ₀ − gt

Concept Check

Why is horizontal velocity constant in projectile motion?

Because no horizontal force acts (ignoring air resistance).

What causes vertical acceleration?

Gravity causes vertical acceleration.

Practice Drills

A projectile has vₓ = 5 m/s and vᵧ = 10 m/s. Find total velocity.

v = √(5² + 10²) = √125 ≈ 11.18 m/s

A ball is thrown horizontally at 8 m/s. What happens to vertical motion?

It accelerates downward due to gravity.

Checkpoint Challenge

A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 30°. Find:
1. Horizontal velocity
2. Vertical velocity

vx = 20 cos30° ≈ 17.32 m/s
vy = 20 sin30° = 10 m/s

Lesson Summary

  • Motion in 2D involves both x and y components.
  • Vectors can be resolved into components.
  • Projectile motion follows a curved path.
  • Horizontal motion is constant; vertical motion is accelerated.
