Chapter 2 Summary: Kinematics

Chapter Overview

This chapter focused on describing motion. Kinematics explains how objects move without first asking what force caused the motion. You studied motion along a straight line, motion in two dimensions, projectile motion, and circular motion.

Big Idea: Kinematics describes motion using position, distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration, vectors, graphs, and equations.

1. Motion Along a Straight Line

Motion occurs when an object changes position over time. In one-dimensional motion, the object moves along a straight path, such as forward and backward, east and west, or up and down.

Term Meaning
Position Location of an object relative to a reference point
Distance Total path traveled; scalar
Displacement Change in position from start to end; vector
Speed Distance divided by time; scalar
Velocity Displacement divided by time; vector
Acceleration Rate of change of velocity

Example: A student walks 8 m east and then 3 m west.

  • Distance = 8 m + 3 m = 11 m
  • Displacement = 8 m east − 3 m west = 5 m east

2. Speed, Velocity, and Acceleration

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
Acceleration = Change in Velocity ÷ Time
Remember:
  • Speed does not need direction.
  • Velocity must include direction.
  • Acceleration happens when velocity changes.
  • An object can accelerate even if its speed is constant, as long as its direction changes.

Example: A bicycle speeds up from 3 m/s to 15 m/s in 6 s.

a = (15 − 3) ÷ 6 = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 m/s²

3. Motion Graphs

Graphs help us describe motion visually.

Graph What the Slope Means
Position-Time Graph Slope represents velocity
Velocity-Time Graph Slope represents acceleration
Graph Reminders:
  • A horizontal position-time graph means the object is at rest.
  • A straight sloping position-time graph means constant velocity.
  • A rising velocity-time graph means positive acceleration.
  • A horizontal velocity-time graph means constant velocity.

4. Motion in 2–3 Dimensions

Real-life motion often occurs in more than one direction. To analyze this, vectors can be separated into horizontal and vertical components.

vx = v cosθ
vy = v sinθ

A vector can be resolved into horizontal and vertical components.

Example: A projectile is launched at 20 m/s at 30°.

  • vx = 20 cos30° ≈ 17.32 m/s
  • vy = 20 sin30° = 10 m/s

5. Projectile Motion

Projectile motion happens when an object moves through the air under the influence of gravity after being launched. Its path is usually parabolic.

Important Projectile Motion Ideas:
  • Horizontal velocity is constant, ignoring air resistance.
  • Vertical velocity changes because of gravity.
  • Horizontal and vertical motions are independent.
  • At the highest point, vertical velocity is zero.
  • For ideal projectile motion, maximum range occurs near 45°.

Projectile motion follows a curved path because horizontal and vertical motions happen at the same time.

6. Circular Motion

Circular motion occurs when an object moves along a circular path. Even if the speed is constant, the object is still accelerating because its direction changes.

Key Circular Motion Ideas:
  • Velocity is tangent to the circular path.
  • Centripetal acceleration points toward the center.
  • Centripetal force also points toward the center.
  • If centripetal force disappears, the object moves tangent to the circle.
ac = v² / r
Fc = mv² / r

Centripetal force and acceleration point toward the center of the circle.

Example: A 4 kg object moves at 5 m/s in a circle with radius 2 m.

Fc = mv² / r
Fc = (4)(5²) / 2
Fc = (4)(25) / 2
Fc = 50 N

7. Formula Summary

Concept Formula
Speed speed = distance ÷ time
Velocity velocity = displacement ÷ time
Acceleration a = (vf − vi) ÷ t
Horizontal Component vx = v cosθ
Vertical Component vy = v sinθ
Resultant Velocity v = √(vx² + vy²)
Centripetal Acceleration ac = v² / r
Centripetal Force Fc = mv² / r

Review Checkpoint

1. A person walks 12 m north and then 5 m south. What are the distance and displacement?

Distance = 12 m + 5 m = 17 m
Displacement = 12 m north − 5 m south = 7 m north

2. A car increases velocity from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 s. Find acceleration.

a = (30 − 10) ÷ 5 = 4 m/s²

3. A vector has magnitude 15 m/s at 60°. Find its horizontal and vertical components.

vx = 15 cos60° = 7.5 m/s
vy = 15 sin60° ≈ 13.0 m/s

4. A projectile is moving at the top of its path. What is its vertical velocity at that instant?

The vertical velocity is 0 m/s at the highest point.

5. A 2 kg object moves at 6 m/s in a circle of radius 3 m. Find the centripetal force.

Fc = mv² / r
Fc = (2)(6²) / 3 = (2)(36) / 3 = 24 N

Final Chapter Summary

  • Kinematics describes motion without focusing first on the cause of motion.
  • Distance is scalar; displacement is vector.
  • Speed uses distance; velocity uses displacement.
  • Acceleration is a change in velocity over time.
  • Position-time graph slope gives velocity.
  • Velocity-time graph slope gives acceleration.
  • Vectors can be resolved into x and y components.
  • Projectile motion combines horizontal constant velocity and vertical acceleration due to gravity.
  • Circular motion involves changing direction, so acceleration exists even at constant speed.
  • Centripetal acceleration and force always point toward the center of the circle.
