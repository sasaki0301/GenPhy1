Work Done and Power

Learning Objectives

  • Define work in physics.
  • Calculate work using force and displacement.
  • Understand power and rate of doing work.
  • Apply concepts to real-life situations.

1. What is Work?

In physics, work is done when a force causes an object to move in the direction of the force.

Key Idea: Work is done only when there is both force and displacement in the same direction.

Work is done when force causes displacement

2. Formula for Work

W = F × d

Where:

  • W = Work (Joules)
  • F = Force (Newtons)
  • d = Displacement (meters)
Important: Work is maximum when force and displacement are in the same direction.

3. Work with Angle

If the force is applied at an angle, only the component in the direction of motion does work.

W = Fd cosθ

Only the horizontal component contributes to work

4. Types of Work

Type Description
Positive Work Force and motion in same direction
Negative Work Force opposite to motion (e.g., friction)
Zero Work No displacement or force perpendicular

Examples:

  • Pushing a box → Positive work
  • Friction slowing motion → Negative work
  • Holding a bag → Zero work

5. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done.

P = W / t
Unit: Watt (W) = Joules per second

Example:

Work = 200 J done in 10 s
Power = 200 ÷ 10 = 20 W

Simulation Task 1: Place the skater at a high point on the track. What type of energy is greatest before the skater starts moving?

Potential energy is greatest because the skater is at a high position. As the skater moves downward, potential energy is converted into kinetic energy.

Simulation Task 2: Turn on friction. What happens to some of the mechanical energy?

Some mechanical energy is converted into thermal energy due to friction. This is why the skater may not return to the same height.

Work is a way of transferring energy. When forces act over a distance, they can change the energy of an object.

6. Real-Life Applications

  • Engines doing work to move cars
  • Athletes lifting weights
  • Electric appliances using power
  • Machines designed for efficiency

Practice Drills

A force of 10 N moves an object 5 m. Find work.

W = 10 × 5 = 50 J

A machine does 300 J of work in 6 s. Find power.

P = 300 ÷ 6 = 50 W

Does carrying a bag horizontally do work on the bag?

No. The force is vertical while displacement is horizontal, so work is zero.

Checkpoint Challenge

A force of 20 N is applied at 60° to move an object 10 m. Find the work done.

W = Fd cosθ
W = 20 × 10 × cos60
W = 200 × 0.5 = 100 J

Lesson Summary

  • Work requires both force and displacement.
  • Work depends on direction of force.
  • Work can be positive, negative, or zero.
  • Power measures how fast work is done.
  • Efficiency and energy use depend on power.
