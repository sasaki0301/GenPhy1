Conservation of Energy

Learning Objectives

  • Define kinetic and potential energy.
  • Understand conservation of mechanical energy.
  • Apply energy equations to solve problems.
  • Relate work to energy changes.

1. What is Energy?

Energy is the ability to do work. It allows objects to move, change, or perform tasks.

Key Idea: Work and energy are closely related—doing work transfers energy.

2. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion.

KE = ½mv²

Example:

A 2 kg object moving at 4 m/s:

KE = ½(2)(4²) = 1 × 16 = 16 J

3. Potential Energy

Potential energy is stored energy due to position.

PE = mgh

Example:

A 3 kg object at height 5 m:

PE = (3)(9.8)(5) = 147 J

4. Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed—it only changes form.

Total Energy = KE + PE
Important: In the absence of friction, total mechanical energy remains constant.

Energy transforms between potential and kinetic energy

5. Energy Transformation

As an object moves:

  • At highest point → Maximum PE, minimum KE
  • At lowest point → Maximum KE, minimum PE

Example:

A ball dropped from height:

  • Top: High PE, low KE
  • Falling: PE decreases, KE increases
  • Bottom: PE lowest, KE highest

6. Work-Energy Connection

Work done on an object changes its energy.

Work = Change in Energy
Key Idea: Positive work increases energy, negative work decreases it.

7. Non-Conservative Forces

Friction and air resistance cause energy loss (usually as heat).

  • Total energy is still conserved
  • Mechanical energy is not conserved

Practice Drills

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s. Find KE.

KE = ½(1)(36) = 18 J

An object has PE = 200 J at top. What is KE at bottom (no friction)?

KE = 200 J

A 2 kg object is lifted 3 m. Find PE (g=10).

PE = 2×10×3 = 60 J

Checkpoint Challenge

A 5 kg object is dropped from 10 m. Find velocity before hitting ground (ignore friction).

PE = KE
mgh = ½mv²
10×10 = ½v²
100 = ½v²
v² = 200
v ≈ 14.14 m/s

Lesson Summary

  • Energy is the ability to do work.
  • Kinetic energy depends on motion.
  • Potential energy depends on position.
  • Energy is conserved in a system.
  • Work transfers energy.
  • Friction converts mechanical energy into other forms.
