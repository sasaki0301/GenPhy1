Momentum and Impulse

Learning Objectives

  • Define momentum and explain why it depends on mass and velocity.
  • Calculate momentum using mass and velocity.
  • Define impulse as force acting over time.
  • Use the impulse-momentum theorem to solve problems.
  • Interpret simple force-time graphs.
  • Explain real-life safety applications such as airbags, seatbelts, and padded surfaces.

1. Why Momentum Matters

Some moving objects are harder to stop than others. A slowly moving truck may be harder to stop than a fast-moving bicycle because the truck has much more mass. Momentum helps us describe how difficult it is to stop or change the motion of an object.

Key Idea: Momentum describes the quantity of motion an object has. It depends on both mass and velocity.

A moving object with greater mass or greater velocity has greater momentum.

2. Momentum

Momentum is the product of an object’s mass and velocity.

p = mv

Where:

  • p = momentum, measured in kg·m/s
  • m = mass, measured in kg
  • v = velocity, measured in m/s
Important: Momentum is a vector quantity because velocity has direction.

Example 1: Calculating Momentum

A 6 kg object moves east at 4 m/s. Find its momentum.

p = mv
p = (6)(4)
p = 24 kg·m/s east

The direction is included because momentum follows the direction of velocity.

3. Direction and Sign of Momentum

Since momentum is a vector, direction matters. In one-dimensional problems, we often assign signs to directions.

Common Sign Convention:
  • Right or east = positive
  • Left or west = negative

Example 2: Momentum with Direction

A 3 kg cart moves left at 5 m/s. If right is positive, what is its momentum?

Since the cart moves left, velocity is negative:
v = -5 m/s

p = mv
p = (3)(-5)
p = -15 kg·m/s

The negative sign means the momentum is directed left.

4. Impulse

Impulse is the effect of a force applied over a period of time. A small force acting for a long time or a large force acting for a short time can both change an object’s momentum.

J = FΔt

Where:

  • J = impulse, measured in N·s
  • F = force, measured in N
  • Δt = time interval, measured in s
Key Idea: Impulse changes momentum.

Example 3: Calculating Impulse

A force of 40 N acts on a ball for 0.20 s. Find the impulse.

J = FΔt
J = (40)(0.20)
J = 8 N·s

5. Impulse-Momentum Theorem

The impulse-momentum theorem states that impulse equals the change in momentum.

J = Δp
FΔt = mvf - mvi
Meaning: A force acting over time changes an object’s momentum.

Example 4: Finding Change in Momentum

A 0.50 kg ball speeds up from 2 m/s to 10 m/s. Find its change in momentum.

Δp = mvf - mvi
Δp = (0.50)(10) - (0.50)(2)
Δp = 5 - 1
Δp = 4 kg·m/s

So the impulse delivered to the ball is also 4 N·s.

Example 5: Finding Average Force

A 0.20 kg ball moving at 15 m/s is stopped by a glove in 0.10 s. Find the average force on the ball.

Initial momentum:
pi = (0.20)(15) = 3 kg·m/s

Final momentum:
pf = 0

Change in momentum:
Δp = 0 - 3 = -3 kg·m/s

Using J = FΔt:
F = Δp / Δt
F = -3 / 0.10
F = -30 N

The negative sign means the force acts opposite the ball’s original motion.

6. Force-Time Graphs

A force-time graph shows how force changes over time. The area under a force-time graph represents impulse.

Graph Idea: Area under force-time graph = impulse.

The area under a force-time graph gives impulse.

Example 6: Rectangular Force-Time Graph

A constant force of 12 N acts for 3 s. Find the impulse.

Impulse = area under graph
J = FΔt
J = (12)(3)
J = 36 N·s

Example 7: Triangular Force-Time Graph

A force-time graph forms a triangle with height 20 N and base 0.40 s. Find the impulse.

Impulse = area of triangle
J = ½(base)(height)
J = ½(0.40)(20)
J = 4 N·s

7. Safety Applications of Impulse

In collisions, the change in momentum may be fixed. For example, a moving person in a car must eventually be brought to rest during a crash. The key is to increase the stopping time so that the average force becomes smaller.

F = Δp / Δt
Key Idea: Increasing collision time reduces average impact force.
Safety Device How It Helps
Airbag Increases stopping time and spreads force over a larger area
Seatbelt Increases stopping time and prevents sudden forward motion
Padded floor Increases collision time when someone falls
Boxing gloves Increase contact time and reduce peak force

Important Application:

A person landing on a soft mat experiences a smaller average force than landing on concrete because the mat increases the stopping time. The change in momentum may be similar, but the force becomes smaller.

Concept Check

1. Is momentum a scalar or vector quantity?

Momentum is a vector quantity because it depends on velocity, which has direction.

2. What happens to momentum if velocity doubles while mass stays the same?

Momentum doubles because p = mv.

3. Why do airbags reduce injury during collisions?

Airbags increase stopping time. Since F = Δp / Δt, increasing time reduces the average force.

4. What does the area under a force-time graph represent?

The area under a force-time graph represents impulse.

Checkpoint Challenge

A 0.10 kg ball moving at 20 m/s is brought to rest in 0.05 s.

1. What is the change in momentum?
2. What is the average force on the ball?

Initial momentum:
pi = (0.10)(20) = 2 kg·m/s

Final momentum:
pf = 0

Change in momentum:
Δp = 0 - 2 = -2 kg·m/s

Average force:
F = Δp / Δt
F = -2 / 0.05 = -40 N

The negative sign means the force acts opposite the original motion.

Challenge Question: Two identical balls have the same speed. One hits a hard wall and stops almost instantly. The other hits a soft cushion and stops slowly. Which one experiences a smaller average force, and why?

The ball hitting the soft cushion experiences a smaller average force because the stopping time is longer. The change in momentum is similar, but increasing time reduces force.

Lesson Summary

  • Momentum is the quantity of motion of an object.
  • Momentum is calculated using p = mv.
  • Momentum is a vector and follows the direction of velocity.
  • Impulse is force acting over time: J = FΔt.
  • Impulse equals change in momentum: J = Δp.
  • The area under a force-time graph represents impulse.
  • Increasing collision time reduces average force.
  • Airbags, seatbelts, and padded surfaces reduce injury by increasing stopping time.
