Collisions

Learning Objectives

  • Understand conservation of momentum in collisions.
  • Differentiate elastic and inelastic collisions.
  • Solve one-dimensional collision problems.
  • Analyze explosions and recoil situations.
  • Interpret momentum changes during interactions.

1. What Happens During a Collision?

A collision occurs when two or more objects interact with each other over a short period of time. During collisions, forces act between objects and momentum is transferred.

Key Idea: In an isolated system, total momentum before collision equals total momentum after collision.

Momentum is transferred during collisions.

2. Conservation of Momentum

The law of conservation of momentum states:

Total Momentum Before = Total Momentum After
m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = m₁v₁' + m₂v₂'
Important: Momentum is conserved only in isolated systems where external forces are negligible.

Example 1: Basic Momentum Conservation

A 2 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides with a stationary 3 kg cart. After collision, the two carts stick together. Find their final velocity.

Initial momentum:
p = (2)(4) + (3)(0)
p = 8 kg·m/s

Since they stick together:

(2+3)v = 8
5v = 8
v = 1.6 m/s

3. Elastic Collisions

In elastic collisions:

  • Momentum is conserved.
  • Kinetic energy is also conserved.
Examples: Collisions between billiard balls or ideal atomic particles.

Objects bounce apart after an elastic collision.

Quantity Elastic Collision
Momentum Conserved
Kinetic Energy Conserved

4. Inelastic Collisions

In inelastic collisions:

  • Momentum is conserved.
  • Kinetic energy is NOT conserved.
Perfectly Inelastic Collision: Objects stick together after collision.

Objects stick together in a perfectly inelastic collision.

Quantity Inelastic Collision
Momentum Conserved
Kinetic Energy Not Conserved

5. Explosions and Recoil

In explosions, objects initially at rest move apart after interaction. Total momentum is still conserved.

Important: Objects move in opposite directions so total momentum remains zero if initial momentum was zero.

Example 2: Recoil

A 4 kg cannon fires a 1 kg cannonball at 12 m/s to the right. Find the recoil velocity of the cannon.

Initial momentum:
0

After firing:

(1)(12) + (4)v = 0

12 + 4v = 0
4v = -12
v = -3 m/s

The negative sign means the cannon moves backward.

6. Kinetic Energy in Collisions

Even though momentum is always conserved, kinetic energy may or may not be conserved.

  • Elastic collision → KE conserved
  • Inelastic collision → KE converted into sound, heat, deformation, etc.

A car crash often produces sound, heat, and deformation. That is why kinetic energy decreases during inelastic collisions.

Practice Drills

A 5 kg object moving at 2 m/s collides and sticks to a 5 kg stationary object. Find final velocity.

Initial momentum:
p = (5)(2)=10

Final:
(10)v = 10
v = 1 m/s

A 2 kg cart moving at 6 m/s collides with a stationary 1 kg cart. If they stick together, find final velocity.

Initial momentum:
p = (2)(6)=12

Final:
3v = 12
v = 4 m/s

Why is a car crash considered an inelastic collision?

Because kinetic energy is transformed into sound, heat, and deformation.

Checkpoint Challenge

A 3 kg cart moving right at 5 m/s collides and sticks to a 2 kg cart moving left at 1 m/s. Find their final velocity.

Take right as positive.

Initial momentum:
p = (3)(5) + (2)(-1)
p = 15 - 2
p = 13

Final:
(3+2)v = 13
5v = 13
v = 2.6 m/s right

Pisay-Style Question: Two identical carts move toward each other with equal speeds. After collision, both stop completely. What type of collision likely occurred and why?

This is likely a perfectly inelastic collision because the carts stick together or stop after collision. Their equal and opposite momenta cancel each other.

Lesson Summary

  • Momentum is conserved in isolated systems.
  • Collisions transfer momentum between objects.
  • Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy.
  • Inelastic collisions conserve momentum but not kinetic energy.
  • Perfectly inelastic collisions cause objects to stick together.
  • Explosions and recoil also obey conservation of momentum.
