Center of Mass and Collision Applications

Learning Objectives

  • Determine the center of mass of simple systems.

  • Understand recoil and explosion motion.

1. What is Center of Mass?

The center of mass is the point where the mass of a system appears to be concentrated. It is the balance point of an object or system.

Key Idea: The motion of a system can be analyzed as if all the mass were concentrated at the center of mass.

The center of mass acts as the balance point of the system.

A ruler balances at its center because its mass is distributed evenly.

2. Center of Mass Formula

For objects along a straight line:

xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂) / (m₁ + m₂)

Where:

  • xcm = center of mass position
  • m = mass
  • x = position

Example 1

A 2 kg mass is located at x = 1 m and a 4 kg mass is located at x = 5 m. Find the center of mass.

xcm = [(2)(1) + (4)(5)] / (2+4)

xcm = (2 + 20) / 6

xcm = 3.67 m

Momentum conservation allows prediction of motion after interactions.

4. Recoil Motion

Recoil occurs when one object moves forward and another moves backward due to momentum conservation.

Total Momentum Before = Total Momentum After

Forward motion produces equal opposite recoil motion.

Example 2

A 60 kg person standing on a frictionless surface throws a 3 kg object at 10 m/s to the right. Find the recoil velocity of the person.

Initial momentum = 0

(3)(10) + (60)v = 0

30 + 60v = 0

v = -0.5 m/s

The person recoils at 0.5 m/s to the left.

5. Explosions

Explosions occur when an object breaks apart into multiple pieces. Momentum remains conserved during the explosion.

If the system initially has zero momentum, the vector sum of momenta after explosion must also equal zero.

Explosion fragments move in different directions while conserving momentum.

Example 3

A stationary object explodes into two fragments. One fragment has momentum 12 kg·m/s east. What is the momentum of the other fragment?

The second fragment must have:

12 kg·m/s west

so total momentum remains zero.

6. Multi-Object Systems

In systems involving several objects:

  • Total momentum is conserved.
  • Momentum is added vectorially.
  • Direction must always be considered.
Always assign positive and negative directions consistently.

= (10+18)/8

= 3.5 m

The other fragment moves west with equal momentum.

Why does a heavy truck change velocity less during collision than a small car?

Because the truck has greater mass and momentum.

Checkpoint Challenge

A 4 kg object moving right at 5 m/s collides with a 2 kg stationary object. After collision, the 4 kg object moves at 2 m/s right. Find the final velocity of the 2 kg object.

Initial momentum:
(4)(5)=20

Final momentum:
(4)(2)+(2)v
8+2v

20 = 8 + 2v
12 = 2v
v = 6 m/s right

Pisay-Style Question: Two skaters push each other apart on frictionless ice. The lighter skater moves faster. Explain why.

Momentum must remain conserved. The lighter skater needs a larger velocity to balance momentum with the heavier skater.

Lesson Summary

  • The center of mass is the balance point of a system.
  • Momentum is conserved in isolated systems.
  • Recoil and explosions obey momentum conservation.
  • Mass strongly affects collision outcomes.
  • Momentum concepts explain many real-world systems.
