Chapter 4 Summary: Momentum, Impulse, and Collisions

Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we explored how motion changes during interactions between objects. We studied momentum, impulse, collisions, recoil, explosions, and center of mass systems.

Big Idea: Momentum is always conserved in isolated systems, even during collisions and explosions.

1. Momentum

p = mv
Quantity Description
Momentum Quantity of motion of an object
Mass Amount of matter
Velocity Speed with direction
Momentum is a vector quantity because velocity has direction.

2. Impulse

J = FΔt
J = Δp

Impulse changes momentum. A larger force or longer interaction time produces greater impulse.

Increasing collision time reduces impact force.
Examples:
  • Airbags
  • Seatbelts
  • Boxing gloves
  • Padded floors

3. Force-Time Graphs

The area under a force-time graph represents impulse.

Impulse = Area Under Curve
Rectangular area:
Area = base × height

Triangular area:
Area = ½(base)(height)

4. Collisions

Total Momentum Before = Total Momentum After
Collision Type Momentum Kinetic Energy
Elastic Conserved Conserved
Inelastic Conserved Not Conserved
Perfectly Inelastic Conserved Not Conserved
Objects stick together in perfectly inelastic collisions.

5. Recoil and Explosions

Recoil occurs when one object moves forward while another moves backward. Explosions separate objects while conserving total momentum.

If initial momentum is zero, final total momentum must also equal zero.

6. Center of Mass

The center of mass is the balance point of a system.

xcm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)/(m₁+m₂)
The center of mass motion depends on external forces acting on the system.

Review Checkpoint

1. A 3 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Find momentum.

p = mv
p = (3)(4)
p = 12 kg·m/s

2. A force of 20 N acts for 0.5 s. Find impulse.

J = FΔt
J = (20)(0.5)
J = 10 N·s

3. Why do airbags reduce injuries?

Airbags increase stopping time, reducing average force.

4. Which collision conserves kinetic energy?

Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy.

5. A system initially has zero momentum. After explosion, one fragment has momentum 8 kg·m/s east. What is the momentum of the second fragment?

8 kg·m/s west

Final Chapter Summary

  • Momentum describes the quantity of motion.
  • Impulse changes momentum.
  • Momentum is conserved in isolated systems.
  • Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy.
  • Inelastic collisions conserve momentum only.
  • Recoil and explosions follow momentum conservation.
  • The center of mass acts as the balance point of a system.
