Chapter 5 Summary — Rotational Motion and Gravitation

Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we studied rotational motion, torque, gravity, and planetary motion. We explored how rotational systems behave, how gravity acts between masses, and how Kepler’s Laws explain planetary orbits.

Big Idea: Rotation and gravity govern the motion of planets, satellites, wheels, and many real-world systems.

1. Rotational Motion

Rotational motion occurs when an object spins around an axis. Examples include wheels, fans, gears, planets, and spinning skaters.

Linear Motion Rotational Motion
Mass Moment of inertia
Velocity Angular velocity
Force Torque
Kinetic Energy Rotational Kinetic Energy

2. Rotational Inertia

Rotational inertia is the resistance to changes in rotational motion.

I = mr²
Mass farther from the axis increases rotational inertia.

Objects with larger rotational inertia are harder to rotate.

3. Torque (Moment of Force)

Torque is the turning effect of force.

τ = rF sinθ
  • Greater force produces greater torque.
  • Greater distance from pivot produces greater torque.
  • Perpendicular force produces maximum torque.
Pushing farther from the pivot makes rotation easier.

4. Rotational Kinetic Energy

KErot = ½Iω²

Rotating objects possess rotational kinetic energy.

Rolling objects possess both translational and rotational kinetic energy.

5. Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation

F = G(m₁m₂ / r²)

Every object with mass attracts every other object through gravity.

Factor Effect on Gravity
Larger masses Increase gravity
Greater distance Decrease gravity
Gravity follows an inverse square relationship.

6. Weight and Gravitational Force

W = mg
  • Mass remains constant.
  • Weight changes depending on gravity.
Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

7. Kepler’s Laws of Planetary Motion

First Law

Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.

Second Law

Planets sweep equal areas in equal times. They move faster near the Sun and slower farther away.

Third Law

T² ∝ r³

Planets farther from the Sun have longer orbital periods.

8. Orbital Motion

Gravity provides the centripetal force that keeps planets and satellites in orbit.

Satellites remain in continuous free fall around Earth.
Term Meaning
Perihelion Closest point to the Sun
Aphelion Farthest point from the Sun

Review Checkpoint

1. Why does a longer wrench produce greater torque?

A longer wrench increases the distance from the pivot, increasing torque.

2. What happens to gravitational force if distance doubles?

The force becomes one-fourth as large.

3. Why do planets move faster near the Sun?

Gravity is stronger when planets are closer to the Sun.

4. Find the rotational kinetic energy of an object with I = 5 kg·m² and ω = 4 rad/s.

KErot = ½Iω²
KErot = ½(5)(4²)
KErot = 2.5(16)
KErot = 40 J

5. Which planet generally has the longer orbital period: a near planet or a far planet?

A planet farther from the Sun has a longer orbital period.

Final Chapter Summary

  • Rotational motion involves spinning around an axis.
  • Rotational inertia depends on mass distribution.
  • Torque causes rotational motion.
  • Rotating objects possess rotational kinetic energy.
  • Gravity acts between all masses.
  • Gravity decreases rapidly with increasing distance.
  • Gravity keeps planets and satellites in orbit.
  • Kepler’s Laws describe planetary motion.
  • Planets move faster near the Sun and slower farther away.
