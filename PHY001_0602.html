Properties of Sound and Mechanical Waves

Learning Objectives

  • Define waves and mechanical waves.
  • Differentiate transverse and longitudinal waves.
  • Identify wavelength, frequency, amplitude, and wave speed.
  • Apply the wave equation.
  • Explain properties of sound waves.
  • Describe resonance and standing waves.

1. What is a Wave?

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without permanently transferring matter.

Key Idea: Waves transfer energy, not matter.

Examples of waves:

  • Water waves
  • Sound waves
  • Waves on a rope
  • Earthquake waves

Waves transfer energy through oscillations.

2. Mechanical Waves

Mechanical waves require a medium to travel. The medium can be solid, liquid, or gas.

Mechanical waves cannot travel through a vacuum.
Wave Type Needs Medium?
Sound waves Yes
Water waves Yes
Light waves No

3. Transverse Waves

In transverse waves, particles move perpendicular to the direction of wave motion.

In transverse waves, particle motion is perpendicular to wave direction.

Part Description
Crest Highest point of wave
Trough Lowest point of wave

Examples of transverse waves:

  • Waves on a rope
  • Electromagnetic waves
  • Water surface waves

4. Longitudinal Waves

In longitudinal waves, particles move parallel to the direction of wave motion.

Longitudinal waves contain compressions and rarefactions.

Part Description
Compression Particles close together
Rarefaction Particles spread apart

Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical waves.

5. Properties of Waves

Quantity Meaning Unit
Amplitude Maximum displacement m
Wavelength (λ) Distance between identical points m
Frequency (f) Number of waves per second Hz
Period (T) Time for one cycle s
Wave Speed (v) Rate of wave travel m/s

Basic parts and measurements of a wave.

6. Wave Equation

v = fλ
  • v = wave speed
  • f = frequency
  • λ = wavelength
Wave speed depends on both frequency and wavelength.

Example 1

A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find wave speed.

v = fλ
v = (5)(2)
v = 10 m/s

Example 2

A sound wave travels at 340 m/s and has frequency 170 Hz. Find wavelength.

v = fλ
340 = (170)λ
λ = 2 m

7. Properties of Sound

Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave produced by vibrating objects.

Property Related To
Pitch Frequency
Loudness Amplitude
Quality/Timbre Waveform
Higher frequency produces higher pitch.

Sound waves travel through compressions and rarefactions.

8. Resonance

Resonance occurs when an object vibrates at its natural frequency due to an external vibration.

Resonance produces very large vibrations.

Examples of resonance:

  • Pushing a swing at the correct timing
  • Musical instruments
  • Shattering glass using sound

9. Standing Waves

Standing waves form when two waves of the same frequency interfere while traveling in opposite directions.

Standing waves contain nodes and antinodes.

Part Description
Node Point with no motion
Antinode Point with maximum motion

Standing waves are common in guitar strings and air columns.

Checkpoint Challenge

A wave travels at 24 m/s and has frequency 6 Hz. Find wavelength.

v = fλ
24 = (6)λ
λ = 4 m

Pisay-Style Question: Two sound waves travel through air. Wave A has greater amplitude while Wave B has greater frequency. Compare their loudness and pitch.

Wave A is louder because it has greater amplitude. Wave B has higher pitch because it has greater frequency.

Lesson Summary

  • Mechanical waves require a medium.
  • Transverse waves oscillate perpendicular to motion.
  • Longitudinal waves oscillate parallel to motion.
  • Wave speed is calculated using v = fλ.
  • Frequency determines pitch.
  • Amplitude affects loudness.
  • Standing waves contain nodes and antinodes.
  • Resonance occurs when vibrations match natural frequency.
