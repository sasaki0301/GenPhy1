Doppler Effect

Learning Objectives

  • Define the Doppler Effect.
  • Explain how motion affects observed frequency.
  • Differentiate approaching and receding sound sources.
  • Relate frequency changes to pitch changes.
  • Apply Doppler Effect concepts to real-life situations.
  • Introduce redshift and blueshift in astronomy.

1. What is the Doppler Effect?

The Doppler Effect is the apparent change in frequency or pitch of a wave due to relative motion between the source and the observer.

Key Idea: Motion changes the observed frequency of waves.

You may notice that an ambulance siren sounds higher in pitch as it approaches and lower in pitch as it moves away. This is the Doppler Effect.

A moving sound source changes the observed pitch.

2. Approaching Source

When the source moves toward the observer:

  • Wavefronts become compressed.
  • Observed wavelength becomes shorter.
  • Observed frequency becomes higher.
  • The pitch sounds higher.
Approaching source → Higher observed frequency → Higher pitch

Wavefronts compress in front of a moving source.

3. Receding Source

When the source moves away from the observer:

  • Wavefronts spread apart.
  • Observed wavelength becomes longer.
  • Observed frequency becomes lower.
  • The pitch sounds lower.
Receding source → Lower observed frequency → Lower pitch

Wavefronts spread apart behind a moving source.

4. Why Does the Doppler Effect Happen?

The source continues producing waves while moving. Because of this motion:

  • Waves in front become crowded together.
  • Waves behind become stretched apart.
The wave speed in the medium remains the same, but wavelength and observed frequency change.

5. Doppler Effect and Sound

Situation Observed Pitch
Source approaching Higher pitch
Source moving away Lower pitch

Example 1

A race car approaches a spectator. The engine sound appears higher in pitch before passing the observer. After passing, the pitch suddenly becomes lower.

An ambulance siren demonstrates the Doppler Effect.

6. Doppler Effect in Astronomy

The Doppler Effect also applies to light waves. Astronomers use it to determine whether stars and galaxies are moving toward or away from Earth.

Term Meaning
Blueshift Object moving toward observer
Redshift Object moving away from observer
Redshift helped scientists discover that the universe is expanding.

Light waves shift depending on motion of stars and galaxies.

7. Simplified Doppler Equation

A simplified Doppler equation for moving observers or sources is:

f' = f(v ± vo)/(v ∓ vs)
  • f' = observed frequency
  • f = actual frequency
  • v = wave speed
  • vo = observer speed
  • vs = source speed
Signs depend on whether source and observer move toward or away from each other.

Conceptual Example

If the source approaches the observer, the denominator decreases, causing observed frequency to increase.

Lesson Summary

  • The Doppler Effect is the apparent change in frequency due to motion.
  • Approaching sources produce higher observed frequency and pitch.
  • Receding sources produce lower observed frequency and pitch.
  • Wavefront spacing changes because the source moves while emitting waves.
  • The Doppler Effect applies to sound and light waves.
  • Redshift and blueshift help astronomers study motion in space.
