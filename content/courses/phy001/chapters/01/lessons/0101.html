Physics and Measurement

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what physics is and why measurement is important.
  • Identify common physical quantities and their SI units.
  • Distinguish between scalar and vector quantities.
  • Convert units using dimensional analysis.
  • Recognize the importance of accuracy, precision, and significant figures.

1. What is Physics?

Physics is the branch of science that studies matter, energy, motion, forces, and the interactions between them. It helps us describe and explain how objects move, why things fall, how machines work, how sound travels, and even how planets move in space.

Key Idea: Physics uses measurements, mathematics, and logical reasoning to describe how the natural world behaves.

For example, when a ball rolls down a ramp, physics can help us answer questions such as:

  • How fast is the ball moving?
  • What force caused it to accelerate?
  • How much energy does it have?
  • How far will it travel after a given time?

2. Why Measurement Matters in Physics

Physics is not only about observing what happens. It is also about describing what happens using numbers. A statement like “the car is fast” is vague. But saying “the car is moving at 25 m/s” gives a measurable and useful description.

Example:

Instead of saying:
“The table is long.”

A better scientific statement is:
“The table has a length of 1.50 meters.”

A measurement must include two parts:
  • A numerical value
  • A unit

For example, in the measurement 12 meters, the number is 12 and the unit is meters.

3. SI Units in Physics

Scientists use the International System of Units, or SI, so that measurements are consistent everywhere. The SI system allows scientists, engineers, and students to communicate measurements clearly.

Physical Quantity SI Unit Symbol
Length meter m
Mass kilogram kg
Time second s
Temperature kelvin K
Electric Current ampere A
Amount of Substance mole mol
Luminous Intensity candela cd
Important: In most General Physics 1 problems, the most frequently used base quantities are length, mass, and time.

4. Derived Quantities

Some physical quantities are formed by combining base quantities. These are called derived quantities.

Derived Quantity Formula or Meaning SI Unit
Area length × width m²
Volume length × width × height m³
Speed distance ÷ time m/s
Acceleration change in velocity ÷ time m/s²
Force mass × acceleration newton, N
Key Idea: Derived units come from combining SI base units.

5. Scalar and Vector Quantities

In physics, some quantities only need a size or amount. Others need both size and direction.

Scalar Quantities

A scalar quantity has magnitude only. Magnitude means size or amount.

  • Mass = 5 kg
  • Time = 10 s
  • Temperature = 30°C
  • Speed = 20 m/s

Vector Quantities

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.

  • Displacement = 15 m east
  • Velocity = 20 m/s north
  • Force = 50 N downward
  • Acceleration = 9.8 m/s² downward

Example:

Speed and velocity are related, but they are not exactly the same.

  • Speed: 10 m/s
  • Velocity: 10 m/s east

Velocity includes direction, so it is a vector.

6. Unit Conversion Using Dimensional Analysis

Dimensional analysis is a method of converting one unit to another by multiplying by conversion factors. A conversion factor is a fraction equal to 1.

Common Conversion Factors:
  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 hour = 60 minutes
  • 1 minute = 60 seconds
  • 1 kg = 1000 g

Step-by-Step Example 1: Convert kilometers to meters

Problem: Convert 3.5 km to meters.

Step 1: Use the conversion factor:

1 km = 1000 m

Step 2: Multiply:

3.5 km × 1000 m / 1 km

Step 3: Cancel km:

3.5 × 1000 = 3500 m

Final Answer: 3500 m

Step-by-Step Example 2: Convert hours to seconds

Problem: Convert 2 hours to seconds.

Step 1: Use conversion factors:

  • 1 hour = 60 minutes
  • 1 minute = 60 seconds

Step 2: Multiply:

2 h × 60 min / 1 h × 60 s / 1 min

Step 3: Cancel units:

2 × 60 × 60 = 7200 s

Final Answer: 7200 s

7. Accuracy and Precision

Measurements are never perfect. That is why scientists describe how close or consistent measurements are.

Accuracy

Accuracy refers to how close a measurement is to the accepted or true value.

Precision

Precision refers to how close repeated measurements are to one another.

Example:

Suppose the true mass of an object is 50.0 g.

  • Measurements of 49.9 g, 50.0 g, and 50.1 g are accurate and precise.
  • Measurements of 45.0 g, 45.1 g, and 45.2 g are precise but not accurate.
  • Measurements of 48 g, 52 g, and 55 g are not very precise.
Key Idea: Accuracy is about correctness. Precision is about consistency.

8. Significant Figures

Significant figures show the meaningful digits in a measurement. They reflect the precision of the measuring tool.

Simple Rule: The more precise the measuring instrument, the more significant figures the measurement usually has.
Measurement Number of Significant Figures Explanation
25.4 m 3 All nonzero digits count.
0.0062 kg 2 Leading zeros do not count.
3.00 s 3 Trailing zeros after a decimal count.
1500 m Ambiguous Without a decimal, trailing zeros may or may not be significant.
Reminder: In calculations, your final answer should usually match the precision of the given data.

Concept Check

1. Why is measurement important in physics?

Measurement allows us to describe physical quantities using numbers and units. This makes observations more exact, testable, and useful.

2. Is mass a scalar or vector quantity?

Mass is a scalar quantity because it has magnitude only and does not need direction.

3. What is the difference between speed and velocity?

Speed has magnitude only, while velocity has both magnitude and direction.

Checkpoint Challenge

A student measures the length of a table three times and records the following values:

  • 1.52 m
  • 1.51 m
  • 1.53 m

The accepted length of the table is 1.80 m.

Answer the following:
1. Are the measurements precise?
2. Are the measurements accurate?
3. Explain your reasoning.

1. Yes, the measurements are precise because they are close to one another.

2. No, the measurements are not accurate because they are far from the accepted value of 1.80 m.

3. The values are consistent, but they are not close to the true value.

Challenge Problem: A cyclist travels 18 km in 30 minutes. Convert the distance to meters and the time to seconds. Then find the cyclist’s average speed in m/s.

Convert distance:
18 km × 1000 = 18,000 m

Convert time:
30 min × 60 = 1800 s

Average speed:
speed = distance ÷ time
speed = 18,000 m ÷ 1800 s
speed = 10 m/s

Lesson Summary

  • Physics studies matter, energy, motion, forces, and interactions.
  • Measurements must include a number and a unit.
  • The SI system provides standard units used in science.
  • Scalars have magnitude only, while vectors have magnitude and direction.
  • Dimensional analysis helps convert units correctly.
  • Accuracy means closeness to the true value, while precision means consistency.
  • Significant figures show the meaningful digits in a measurement.
