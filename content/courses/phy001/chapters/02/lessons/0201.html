Kinematics: Motion Along a Straight Line

Learning Objectives

  • Define motion, position, distance, and displacement.
  • Distinguish between speed and velocity.
  • Calculate average speed, average velocity, and acceleration.
  • Interpret simple position-time and velocity-time graphs.

1. What is Motion?

Motion occurs when an object changes its position relative to a reference point over time. The reference point acts as the starting location used to describe where the object is.

Key Idea: Motion means a change in position over time.

An object moves when its position changes relative to a reference point.

2. Position, Distance, and Displacement

Position

Position tells where an object is located relative to a reference point.

Distance

Distance is the total path traveled by an object. It does not include direction, so it is a scalar quantity.

Displacement

Displacement is the change in position from the starting point to the ending point. It includes direction, so it is a vector quantity.

Distance vs. Displacement:
  • Distance = total path traveled
  • Displacement = final position − initial position

Distance follows the path taken; displacement only compares start and end positions.

Example: A person walks 5 m east, then 3 m west.

  • Distance = 5 m + 3 m = 8 m
  • Displacement = 5 m east − 3 m west = 2 m east

3. Speed and Velocity

Speed

Speed tells how fast an object moves.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Velocity

Velocity tells how fast an object moves and in what direction.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
Remember:
  • Speed is scalar.
  • Velocity is vector.

Example: A car travels 100 m east in 10 s.

Speed = 100 m ÷ 10 s = 10 m/s
Velocity = 100 m east ÷ 10 s = 10 m/s east

4. Position-Time Graphs

A position-time graph shows how an object’s position changes as time passes. The slope of a position-time graph represents velocity.

Graph Idea: A steeper position-time graph means greater velocity.

A straight diagonal line means constant velocity.

Interpretation:

If the line rises steadily, the object is moving away from the starting point at a constant velocity. If the line is horizontal, the object is not changing position, so it is at rest.

5. Average Velocity

Average velocity depends on total displacement, not total distance.

Average Velocity = Total Displacement ÷ Total Time

Example:

A student walks 10 m east in 5 s, then walks back 10 m west in 5 s.

  • Total distance = 20 m
  • Total displacement = 0 m
  • Total time = 10 s

Average velocity = 0 m ÷ 10 s = 0 m/s

The student moved, but returned to the starting point, so the displacement is zero.

6. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. An object accelerates when it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction.

Acceleration = Change in Velocity ÷ Time
a = (vf − vi) ÷ t

A rising velocity-time graph means the object is accelerating.

Example: A car increases its velocity from 5 m/s to 15 m/s in 5 s.

a = (15 − 5) ÷ 5
a = 10 ÷ 5
a = 2 m/s²

7. Graph Interpretation Summary

  • Position-time graph: slope tells velocity.
  • Velocity-time graph: slope tells acceleration.
  • Horizontal position-time graph: object is at rest.
  • Horizontal velocity-time graph: object moves at constant velocity.
  • Rising velocity-time graph: object is speeding up.

Concept Check

1. Is distance a scalar or vector?

Distance is a scalar quantity because it has magnitude only.

2. Why can average velocity be zero even if an object moved?

Because average velocity depends on displacement. If the object returns to its starting point, displacement is zero.

3. What does the slope of a position-time graph represent?

The slope of a position-time graph represents velocity.

4. What does the slope of a velocity-time graph represent?

The slope of a velocity-time graph represents acceleration.

Practice Drills

Drill 1: A runner travels 200 m in 20 s. Find the speed.

Speed = distance ÷ time
Speed = 200 m ÷ 20 s = 10 m/s

Drill 2: A car moves 60 m north in 6 s. Find the velocity.

Velocity = displacement ÷ time
Velocity = 60 m north ÷ 6 s = 10 m/s north

Drill 3: A bicycle increases its velocity from 4 m/s to 12 m/s in 4 s. Find acceleration.

a = (vf − vi) ÷ t
a = (12 − 4) ÷ 4
a = 2 m/s²

Drill 4: A student walks 6 m east, then 2 m west. Find the distance and displacement.

Distance = 6 m + 2 m = 8 m
Displacement = 6 m east − 2 m west = 4 m east

Checkpoint Challenge

A car travels 50 m east in 5 s, then 50 m west in 5 s.

1. What is the total distance?
2. What is the displacement?
3. What is the average speed?
4. What is the average velocity?

1. Distance = 50 m + 50 m = 100 m
2. Displacement = 0 m
3. Average speed = 100 m ÷ 10 s = 10 m/s
4. Average velocity = 0 m ÷ 10 s = 0 m/s

Graph Challenge: A position-time graph is a straight line sloping upward from left to right. What does this tell you about the object’s motion?

It means the object is moving away from the reference point at a constant velocity.

Lesson Summary

  • Motion is a change in position over time.
  • Distance is the total path traveled.
  • Displacement is the change from initial position to final position.
  • Speed is distance divided by time.
  • Velocity is displacement divided by time and includes direction.
  • Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
  • The slope of a position-time graph represents velocity.
  • The slope of a velocity-time graph represents acceleration.
