Circular Motion

Learning Objectives

  • Understand uniform circular motion.
  • Describe centripetal acceleration and force.
  • Apply equations for circular motion.
  • Identify real-life applications of circular motion.

1. What is Circular Motion?

Circular motion occurs when an object moves along a circular path. Even if the speed is constant, the direction is constantly changing.

Key Idea: A change in direction means the object is accelerating—even if speed is constant.

Object moving in a circular path

2. Velocity in Circular Motion

Velocity is always tangent to the circle.

Important: Velocity direction changes continuously.

Velocity is tangent to the circular path

3. Centripetal Acceleration

Centripetal acceleration is always directed toward the center of the circle.

a = v² / r
Key Idea: Acceleration points inward (toward center).

Centripetal acceleration always points toward the center

4. Centripetal Force

Centripetal force keeps the object moving in a circular path.

F = mv² / r
  • Greater mass → greater force
  • Greater speed → much greater force
  • Larger radius → smaller force

Centripetal force pulls the object toward the center

5. Real-Life Examples

  • A car turning on a curve
  • A satellite orbiting Earth
  • A ball tied to a string and swung in a circle
  • Planets orbiting the Sun

6. Example Problem

Problem:

A 2 kg object moves in a circle of radius 4 m at 6 m/s. Find the centripetal force.

F = mv² / r
F = (2)(6²) / 4
F = (2)(36) / 4
F = 72 / 4
F = 18 N

Concept Check

Why is an object accelerating in circular motion even at constant speed?

Because its direction is constantly changing.

Where does centripetal force always point?

Toward the center of the circle.

Practice Drills

A 1 kg object moves at 5 m/s in a circle of radius 2 m. Find force.

F = (1)(25)/2 = 12.5 N

A car doubles its speed. What happens to centripetal force?

Force becomes 4 times larger (because of v²).

Checkpoint Challenge

A 1000 kg car moves in a circular path of radius 50 m at 10 m/s. Find the centripetal force.

F = (1000)(100)/50 = 2000 N

Lesson Summary

  • Circular motion involves constant change in direction.
  • Velocity is tangent to the path.
  • Acceleration is directed inward.
  • Centripetal force keeps objects moving in a circle.
  • Force depends on mass, speed, and radius.
