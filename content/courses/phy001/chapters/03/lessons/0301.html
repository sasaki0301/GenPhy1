Newton’s Laws of Motion and Applications

Learning Objectives

  • Understand Newton’s Three Laws of Motion.
  • Identify forces acting on objects.
  • Apply Newton’s Laws to real-life situations.
  • Analyze motion using free-body diagrams.

1. What is Force?

A force is a push or pull that can change an object’s motion. Forces can cause objects to start moving, stop, speed up, slow down, or change direction.

Key Idea: Force causes acceleration.
F = ma

2. Newton’s First Law (Law of Inertia)

An object will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force.

Inertia: The tendency of an object to resist changes in motion.

Objects resist changes in motion due to inertia

Example: Passengers move forward when a car suddenly stops.

3. Newton’s Second Law

The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass.

F = ma
  • More force → more acceleration
  • More mass → less acceleration

Example:

A 5 kg object experiences a force of 20 N.

a = F / m = 20 / 5 = 4 m/s²

4. Newton’s Third Law

For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force.

Important: Forces always occur in pairs.

Action and reaction forces act on different objects

Example:

  • A rocket pushes gas downward.
  • The gas pushes the rocket upward.

5. Free-Body Diagrams

A free-body diagram shows all the forces acting on an object.

Common Forces:
  • Weight (mg)
  • Normal force
  • Friction
  • Tension
  • Applied force

Example of a free-body diagram

Simulation Task 1: Apply equal forces on both sides of the object. What happens to the net force and motion?

The net force becomes zero because the forces are balanced. If the object is at rest, it remains at rest. If it is already moving, it continues moving at constant velocity.

Simulation Task 2: Increase the applied force while keeping the mass the same. What happens to acceleration?

Acceleration increases. According to Newton’s Second Law, acceleration is directly proportional to net force.

Simulation Task 3: Increase the mass while keeping the applied force the same. What happens to acceleration?

Acceleration decreases. According to Newton’s Second Law, acceleration is inversely proportional to mass.

6. Applications

  • Pushing objects (force and acceleration)
  • Friction affecting motion
  • Seatbelts and inertia
  • Rocket propulsion (Newton’s Third Law)

Practice Drills

a = 50 / 10 = 5 m/s²

Lesson Summary

  • Force causes acceleration.
  • Newton’s First Law explains inertia.
  • Newton’s Second Law relates force, mass, and acceleration.
  • Newton’s Third Law describes action-reaction pairs.
  • Free-body diagrams help analyze forces.
