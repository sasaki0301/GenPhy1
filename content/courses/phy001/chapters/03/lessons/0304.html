Chapter 3 Summary: Forces, Work, and Energy

Chapter Overview

This chapter explored how forces cause motion and how energy is transferred and conserved. You learned Newton’s Laws, how to calculate work and power, and how energy transforms in physical systems.

Big Idea: Forces cause motion, work transfers energy, and energy is conserved in all physical processes.

1. Newton’s Laws of Motion

LawDescription
First Law Objects remain at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net force (Inertia).
Second Law Force causes acceleration: F = ma.
Third Law Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Net force determines motion.
  • Balanced forces → no acceleration.
  • Unbalanced forces → acceleration.

2. Work

W = Fd cosθ
TypeMeaning
PositiveForce in direction of motion
NegativeForce opposite motion
ZeroNo displacement or perpendicular force

Key Reminder: No movement → no work done.

3. Power

P = W / t

Power measures how fast work is done.

More work in less time → greater power.

4. Energy

TypeFormula
Kinetic EnergyKE = ½mv²
Potential EnergyPE = mgh
  • Kinetic energy depends on velocity squared.
  • Potential energy depends on height.

5. Conservation of Energy

Total Energy = KE + PE
Energy cannot be created or destroyed—only transformed.
  • Top of motion → max PE
  • Bottom of motion → max KE
  • Energy continuously transforms between forms

6. Non-Conservative Forces

Friction converts mechanical energy into heat.

  • Total energy is conserved
  • Mechanical energy decreases

Review Checkpoint

1. A 5 kg object experiences 20 N. Find acceleration.

a = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s²

2. A force of 10 N moves an object 3 m at 60°. Find work.

W = 10×3×0.5 = 15 J

3. A 2 kg object moves at 4 m/s. Find KE.

KE = ½(2)(16) = 16 J

4. If an object has 100 J at the top, what is its KE at bottom (no friction)?

100 J

5. Why does energy decrease in real systems?

Because friction converts mechanical energy into heat.

Final Chapter Summary

  • Forces cause acceleration (Newton’s Laws).
  • Work transfers energy.
  • Power measures how fast work is done.
  • Energy exists as kinetic and potential forms.
  • Energy is conserved in all systems.
  • Friction converts mechanical energy into other forms.
