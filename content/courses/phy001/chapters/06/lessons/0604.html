Chapter 6 Summary — Oscillations and Waves

Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we explored oscillatory motion, mechanical waves, sound waves, and the Doppler Effect. We learned how objects oscillate around equilibrium positions, how waves transfer energy, how sound behaves as a mechanical wave, and how motion affects the frequency of waves observed by a listener.

Big Idea: Oscillations produce waves, waves transfer energy, and motion can change how waves are observed.

1. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

Simple Harmonic Motion is a special type of oscillation in which the restoring force is proportional to displacement and directed toward equilibrium.

The restoring force always acts toward the equilibrium position.
Term Meaning
Amplitude Maximum displacement from equilibrium
Period (T) Time for one complete oscillation
Frequency (f) Number of oscillations per second
Equilibrium Position Central resting position
f = 1/T
T = 1/f

2. Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

Energy continuously changes between kinetic energy and potential energy during oscillation.

Position Kinetic Energy Potential Energy
Maximum Displacement Minimum Maximum
Equilibrium Maximum Minimum
The oscillating object moves fastest at equilibrium and slowest at the endpoints.

3. Mechanical Waves

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy without permanently transferring matter.

Mechanical waves require a medium to travel.
Wave Type Requires Medium?
Sound Waves Yes
Water Waves Yes
Light Waves No

4. Types of Mechanical Waves

Type Particle Motion Example
Transverse Perpendicular to wave motion Waves on a rope
Longitudinal Parallel to wave motion Sound waves
Transverse waves contain crests and troughs, while longitudinal waves contain compressions and rarefactions.

5. Wave Properties

Quantity Description
Amplitude Maximum displacement
Wavelength (λ) Distance between identical points on consecutive waves
Frequency (f) Number of waves passing each second
Period (T) Time for one wave cycle
Wave Speed (v) Speed of wave propagation
v = fλ
Wave speed equals frequency multiplied by wavelength.

6. Properties of Sound

Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave that travels through a medium using compressions and rarefactions.

Property Determined By
Pitch Frequency
Loudness Amplitude
Timbre Waveform
Higher frequency means higher pitch. Greater amplitude means louder sound.

7. Resonance

Resonance occurs when an object vibrates at its natural frequency because of an external vibration.

Resonance can produce very large oscillations.

Examples:

  • Pushing a swing at the correct timing
  • Musical instruments
  • Vibrating tuning forks
  • Glass breaking due to sound

8. Standing Waves

Standing waves form when two waves of the same frequency travel in opposite directions and interfere.

Part Description
Node No displacement
Antinode Maximum displacement
Standing waves commonly occur in strings and air columns.

9. Doppler Effect

The Doppler Effect is the apparent change in frequency caused by relative motion between a source and an observer.

Situation Observed Frequency Observed Pitch
Source Approaching Higher Higher
Source Receding Lower Lower
Approaching sources compress wavefronts. Receding sources stretch wavefronts.

10. Doppler Effect in Astronomy

Effect Meaning
Blueshift Object moving toward Earth
Redshift Object moving away from Earth
Redshift observations helped scientists discover that the universe is expanding.

Formula Summary

Concept Formula
Frequency and Period f = 1/T
Period and Frequency T = 1/f
Wave Equation v = fλ
Hooke's Law F = -kx
Pendulum Period T = 2π√(L/g)
Spring Period T = 2π√(m/k)

Chapter Review Checkpoint

1. Where is speed greatest in SHM?

Speed is greatest at the equilibrium position.

2. A wave has frequency 8 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find wave speed.

v = fλ = (8)(2) = 16 m/s

3. Which property determines sound pitch?

Frequency determines pitch.

4. What happens to observed pitch when a siren approaches?

Observed pitch becomes higher.

5. What astronomical phenomenon indicates a galaxy is moving away from Earth?

Redshift.

Final Chapter Summary

  • Simple Harmonic Motion is oscillatory motion driven by a restoring force.
  • Oscillating systems continuously exchange kinetic and potential energy.
  • Mechanical waves transfer energy through a medium.
  • Transverse and longitudinal waves differ in particle motion.
  • Wave speed depends on wavelength and frequency.
  • Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave.
  • Frequency determines pitch while amplitude determines loudness.
  • Resonance occurs when vibrations match natural frequency.
  • Standing waves contain nodes and antinodes.
  • The Doppler Effect changes observed frequency because of relative motion.
  • Redshift and blueshift allow astronomers to study the motion of stars and galaxies.
