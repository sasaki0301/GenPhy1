Pressure and Pascal's Principle

Learning Objectives

  • Define pressure and explain its significance.
  • Calculate pressure using force and area.
  • Explain how pressure behaves in fluids.
  • Calculate fluid pressure at various depths.
  • Apply Pascal's Principle to hydraulic systems.
  • Analyze real-world applications involving pressure and hydraulics.

1. Introduction: Why Can a Needle Pierce More Easily?

Suppose you push a sewing needle and the flat side of a coin against a piece of cardboard using the same force. The needle easily penetrates the cardboard while the coin does not.

The reason is not the amount of force applied, but how concentrated that force is. This concept is called pressure.

Pressure measures how concentrated a force is over a given area.

The same force creates different pressures when applied over different areas.

2. What is Pressure?

Pressure is defined as force acting per unit area.

P = F/A
Where:
  • P = Pressure (Pa)
  • F = Force (N)
  • A = Area (m²)
Pressure increases when force increases or when area decreases.

SI Unit of Pressure

1 Pa = 1 N/m²

3. Understanding Pressure

Situation Pressure
Large Force, Small Area High Pressure
Small Force, Large Area Low Pressure

This explains why:

  • Knives have sharp edges.
  • Needles have pointed tips.
  • Snowshoes are wide.
  • Tank tracks are wider than ordinary tires.

4. Sample Problem: Calculating Pressure

Example 1

A force of 240 N acts on an area of 0.08 m². Find the pressure.

P = F/A

P = 240/0.08

P = 3000 Pa

Answer: 3000 Pa

Example 2

A pressure of 8000 Pa acts on an area of 0.05 m². Find the force.

F = PA

F = (8000)(0.05)

F = 400 N

Answer: 400 N

5. Pressure in Fluids

A fluid is any substance that can flow.

Examples:
  • Water
  • Air
  • Oil
  • Gasoline
  • Blood

Unlike solids, fluids exert pressure in all directions.

Fluid pressure increases with depth.

6. Fluid Pressure Equation

P = ρgh
Where:
  • ρ = Density (kg/m³)
  • g = 9.8 m/s²
  • h = Depth (m)

Pressure increases as depth increases.

7. Why Does Pressure Increase with Depth?

As depth increases, more fluid exists above a given point. The weight of this fluid creates additional pressure.

This is why divers feel increasing pressure as they descend deeper underwater.

It is also why dam walls are built thicker at the bottom than at the top.

The lower portion of a dam experiences much greater pressure.

8. Sample Problem: Fluid Pressure

Example 3

A diver is 5 m below the water surface. Calculate the pressure due to water.

ρ = 1000 kg/m³

P = ρgh

P = (1000)(9.8)(5)

P = 49,000 Pa

Answer: 49,000 Pa

9. Pascal's Principle

French scientist Blaise Pascal discovered that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally throughout the fluid.

Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

This principle allows small forces to produce much larger forces in hydraulic systems.

10. Hydraulic Systems

Hydraulic systems use Pascal's Principle to multiply force.

Examples include:
  • Hydraulic lifts
  • Hydraulic brakes
  • Bulldozers
  • Excavators
  • Dentist chairs
F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂

Hydraulic lifts multiply force using Pascal's Principle.

11. Sample Problem: Hydraulic Lift

Example 4

Input piston:

  • Force = 150 N
  • Area = 0.02 m²

Output piston:

  • Area = 0.40 m²

Find the output force.

150/0.02 = F₂/0.40

F₂ = 3000 N

Answer: 3000 N

Checkpoint Challenge

Why do tank tracks exert less pressure on the ground than ordinary vehicle tires?

Tank tracks spread the vehicle's weight over a much larger area, reducing pressure.

Pisay-Style Question: A hydraulic lift has an output piston area 30 times larger than the input piston area. If a mechanic pushes with a force of 120 N, what output force can ideally be produced?

F₂ = 30 × 120

F₂ = 3600 N

Lesson Summary

  • Pressure is force per unit area.
  • Pressure is calculated using P = F/A.
  • Pressure increases when area decreases.
  • Fluid pressure increases with depth.
  • Fluid pressure is calculated using P = ρgh.
  • Pascal's Principle states that pressure is transmitted equally throughout a confined fluid.
  • Hydraulic systems use Pascal's Principle to multiply force.
  • Pressure concepts explain many engineering and everyday applications.
