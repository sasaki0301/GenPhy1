1. Why Do Ships Float?

A steel nail sinks immediately when placed in water, yet enormous steel ships can float across oceans.

How is this possible?

The answer involves a force called buoyant force.

Buoyant force is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it.

2. Understanding Buoyancy

When an object is placed in a fluid, pressure acts on all sides.

Because pressure increases with depth, the bottom of the object experiences greater pressure than the top.

This pressure difference produces an upward force called the buoyant force.

Pressure differences create an upward buoyant force.

3. Archimedes' Principle

Greek mathematician and scientist Archimedes discovered an important relationship between buoyancy and displaced fluid.

The buoyant force acting on an object is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

4. Archimedes' Principle Equation

Fb = ρVg
Where:
  • Fb = Buoyant Force (N)
  • ρ = Density of Fluid (kg/m³)
  • V = Volume of Fluid Displaced (m³)
  • g = Acceleration due to Gravity (9.8 m/s²)
The more fluid displaced, the greater the buoyant force.

5. Floating, Sinking, and Suspension

Condition Result
Buoyant Force > Weight Object Rises
Buoyant Force = Weight Object Floats / Suspended
Buoyant Force < Weight Object Sinks
Whether an object floats or sinks depends on the balance between buoyant force and weight.

6. Density and Floating

Density plays an important role in buoyancy.

Density Comparison Behavior
Object less dense than fluid Floats
Object equal density to fluid Suspended
Object more dense than fluid Sinks

Examples

  • Wood floats in water.
  • Ice floats on water.
  • Steel nails sink.
  • Ships float because their average density is less than water.

7. Sample Problem: Buoyant Force

Example 1

A submerged object displaces 0.02 m³ of water.

Find the buoyant force.

Given:

ρ = 1000 kg/m³

g = 9.8 m/s²

Fb = ρVg

Fb = (1000)(0.02)(9.8)

Fb = 196 N

Answer: 196 N

8. Bernoulli's Principle

Swiss scientist Daniel Bernoulli studied how fluids move.

He discovered a relationship between fluid speed and pressure.

As fluid speed increases, fluid pressure decreases.

9. Understanding Bernoulli's Principle

Imagine water flowing through a pipe.

If the pipe narrows, the water must move faster through the narrow section.

As the speed increases, the pressure decreases.

Faster fluid flow corresponds to lower pressure.

10. Bernoulli's Principle and Airplane Wings

Airplane wings are designed so that air moves faster over the top surface than underneath.

According to Bernoulli's Principle:

  • Faster air above the wing → Lower pressure
  • Slower air below the wing → Higher pressure

The pressure difference creates lift.

Pressure differences create lift on airplane wings.

11. Applications of Bernoulli's Principle

  • Airplane wings
  • Perfume atomizers
  • Carburetors
  • Venturi meters
  • Roofs lifted during strong winds
  • Fast-moving fluids in pipes

Checkpoint Challenge

Why can a massive steel ship float while a small steel nail sinks?

A ship's hollow structure gives it a lower average density than water, allowing sufficient buoyant force to support its weight.

Pisay-Style Question: A submerged object weighs 250 N. The buoyant force acting on it is 300 N. Predict what will happen when released.

The object will rise because the buoyant force is greater than its weight.

Pisay-Style Question: Strong winds pass rapidly over a building's roof. Explain why the roof may be lifted upward.

Fast-moving air above the roof creates lower pressure. Higher pressure below the roof pushes upward, producing lift.

Lesson Summary

  • Buoyant force is the upward force exerted by a fluid.
  • Archimedes' Principle states that buoyant force equals the weight of displaced fluid.
  • Floating and sinking depend on density and force balance.
  • Objects less dense than the fluid float.
  • Bernoulli's Principle states that faster-moving fluids have lower pressure.
  • Pressure differences explain airplane lift and many fluid-flow applications.
  • Archimedes' and Bernoulli's Principles are fundamental concepts in fluid mechanics.
