Heat and Thermal Expansion Calculations

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate heat energy using specific heat capacity.
  • Explain how different materials respond to heating.
  • Solve problems involving heat gain and heat loss.
  • Apply calorimetry concepts.
  • Perform advanced thermal expansion calculations.
  • Analyze real-world engineering situations involving temperature changes.

1. Why Does Water Take Longer to Heat Than Metal?

Suppose you place a metal spoon and a cup of water inside a hot oven for the same amount of time.

The spoon quickly becomes hot, while the water takes much longer to increase in temperature.

Why?

Different materials require different amounts of energy to change temperature.

The amount of heat needed to change temperature depends on the material's specific heat capacity.

2. Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity tells us how much heat energy is required to raise the temperature of 1 kilogram of a substance by 1°C.

Materials with high specific heat require more energy to heat up.
Material Specific Heat (J/kg·°C)
Water 4186
Aluminum 900
Iron 450
Copper 385

Notice that water has a much larger specific heat capacity than most metals.

3. Heat Energy Equation

Q = mcΔT
Where:
  • Q = Heat Energy (J)
  • m = Mass (kg)
  • c = Specific Heat Capacity (J/kg·°C)
  • ΔT = Temperature Change (°C)
This equation is one of the most important formulas in thermal physics.

4. Example: Heating Water

Example 1

How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 20°C to 50°C?

Given:

m = 2 kg

c = 4186 J/kg·°C

ΔT = 30°C

Q = mcΔT

Q = (2)(4186)(30)

Q = 251,160 J

Answer: 251,160 J

5. Heat Gain and Heat Loss

Heat naturally flows from hotter objects to colder objects.

In an isolated system:

Heat Lost = Heat Gained
Energy is conserved during heat transfer.

6. Introduction to Calorimetry

Calorimetry is the measurement of heat transfer.

Scientists use calorimeters to determine heat exchange between substances.

m₁c₁ΔT₁ = m₂c₂ΔT₂

This equation is often used when two objects reach thermal equilibrium.

7. Example: Thermal Equilibrium

Example 2

A hot metal transfers 5000 J of heat to water.

How much heat does the water gain?

Water gains exactly 5000 J because energy is conserved.

8. Review of Thermal Expansion

Most solids expand when heated because their particles move farther apart.

ΔL = αL₀ΔT
Where:
  • ΔL = Change in Length
  • α = Coefficient of Expansion
  • L₀ = Original Length
  • ΔT = Temperature Change

9. Example: Expansion of a Metal Rod

Example 3

A steel rod is 40 m long at 10°C.

The temperature rises to 50°C.

Given:

α = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ /°C

Find the increase in length.

ΔT = 40°C

ΔL = αL₀ΔT

ΔL = (1.2×10⁻⁵)(40)(40)

ΔL = 0.0192 m

ΔL = 1.92 cm

Answer: 1.92 cm

10. Multi-Step Engineering Problems

Challenge Problem 1

A 5 kg aluminum block initially at 25°C absorbs 67,500 J of heat.

Given:

c = 900 J/kg·°C

Find its final temperature.

Q = mcΔT

67,500 = (5)(900)(ΔT)

67,500 = 4500ΔT

ΔT = 15°C

Final Temperature

T = 25 + 15

T = 40°C

Challenge Problem 2

A copper bridge cable 80 m long experiences a temperature increase from 15°C to 55°C.

Given:

α = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ /°C

Find the expansion.

ΔT = 40°C

ΔL = αL₀ΔT

ΔL = (1.7×10⁻⁵)(80)(40)

ΔL = 0.0544 m

ΔL = 5.44 cm

11. Pisay-Style Application Problems

Challenge Problem 3

Two metal rods have equal lengths.

Rod A has twice the coefficient of expansion of Rod B.

Both experience the same temperature increase.

Compare their expansions.

Since ΔL = αL₀ΔT and αA = 2αB, Rod A expands twice as much as Rod B.

Challenge Problem 4

A 2 kg sample of water and a 2 kg sample of iron receive the same amount of heat energy.

Which undergoes the greater temperature increase?

Iron. Because iron has a much smaller specific heat capacity, its temperature rises more for the same heat input.

Lesson Summary

  • Specific heat capacity determines how much energy is needed to change temperature.
  • Heat calculations use Q = mcΔT.
  • Materials with larger specific heat capacities require more energy to heat up.
  • Heat flows from hotter objects to colder objects.
  • In isolated systems, heat lost equals heat gained.
  • Thermal expansion is described by ΔL = αL₀ΔT.
  • Engineers must account for thermal expansion in bridges, cables, railways, and buildings.
  • Thermal calculations are essential in engineering, climate science, manufacturing, and energy systems.
