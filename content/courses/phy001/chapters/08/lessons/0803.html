1. Why Does an Engine Get Hot?

When a car engine runs, fuel burns and releases energy.

Some of this energy is converted into useful work that moves the vehicle, while some becomes heat that warms the engine.

The same thing happens in power plants, refrigerators, air conditioners, and even in the human body.

Thermodynamics studies how heat, work, and energy are related.

2. What is Internal Energy?

Every substance contains energy due to the motion and interactions of its particles.

This energy is called internal energy.

Internal energy is the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of the particles in a system.

Internal energy increases when:

  • The temperature increases.
  • Heat is added.
  • Work is done on the system.

3. Heat and Work Revisited

In thermodynamics, energy can enter or leave a system in two ways:

Energy Transfer Description
Heat (Q) Energy transferred due to temperature difference
Work (W) Energy transferred by forces causing motion
Heat and work are processes that transfer energy. Internal energy is energy stored within the system.

4. First Law of Thermodynamics

The First Law of Thermodynamics is a statement of energy conservation.

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred or transformed.

ΔU = Q − W
Where:
  • ΔU = Change in Internal Energy (J)
  • Q = Heat Added to the System (J)
  • W = Work Done by the System (J)
The increase in internal energy equals heat added minus work done by the system.

5. Understanding the Signs

Quantity Positive When
Q Heat enters the system
W System does work on surroundings
ΔU Internal energy increases

Simple Interpretation

  • Add heat → Internal energy tends to increase.
  • System does work → Internal energy tends to decrease.

6. Gas in a Cylinder

Imagine heating a gas inside a cylinder with a movable piston.

As the gas absorbs heat:

  • The particles move faster.
  • The gas expands.
  • The piston rises.
  • The gas does work.

A heated gas expands and does work on a piston.

7. Example: Change in Internal Energy

Example 1

A gas absorbs 500 J of heat and performs 200 J of work.

Find the change in internal energy.

ΔU = Q − W

ΔU = 500 − 200

ΔU = 300 J

Answer: The internal energy increases by 300 J.

8. Example: Internal Energy Decreases

Example 2

A gas absorbs 400 J of heat while doing 700 J of work.

Find the change in internal energy.

ΔU = Q − W

ΔU = 400 − 700

ΔU = -300 J

Answer: Internal energy decreases by 300 J.

9. Important Thermodynamic Situations

Situation Result
Q = 0 No heat enters or leaves
W = 0 No work is done
ΔU = 0 Internal energy remains constant
Many thermodynamic problems can be simplified by identifying what quantities are zero.

10. Heat Engines

Heat engines convert thermal energy into useful work.

Examples:
  • Car engines
  • Steam engines
  • Jet engines
  • Power plants

Heat engines convert thermal energy into mechanical work.

11. Refrigerators and Air Conditioners

A refrigerator removes heat from a cold region and transfers it to a warmer region.

Unlike heat engines, refrigerators require external work.

Examples:
  • Home refrigerators
  • Freezers
  • Air conditioners
  • Heat pumps
Refrigerators use work to move heat from colder places to warmer places.

12. Thermodynamics in the Human Body

The human body is also a thermodynamic system.

Food provides chemical energy.

This energy can be transformed into:

  • Mechanical work (movement)
  • Heat
  • Stored energy

When exercising, your muscles perform work and produce heat, which is why body temperature rises.

13. Multi-Step Thermodynamics Problems

Challenge Problem 1

A gas absorbs 1500 J of heat and performs 900 J of work.

Find the change in internal energy.

ΔU = Q − W

ΔU = 1500 − 900

ΔU = 600 J

Challenge Problem 2

A gas loses 700 J of heat and has 300 J of work done on it.

Find the change in internal energy.

Heat lost: Q = -700 J

Work done ON the gas: W = -300 J

ΔU = Q − W

ΔU = (-700) - (-300)

ΔU = -400 J

14. Pisay-Style Conceptual Challenges

Challenge Problem 3

Two identical gases absorb the same amount of heat.

Gas A performs more work than Gas B.

Which gas experiences the larger increase in internal energy?

Gas B. Since less energy is used to perform work, more remains as internal energy.

Challenge Problem 4

A system absorbs 1000 J of heat and its internal energy increases by 400 J.

How much work did the system perform?

ΔU = Q − W

400 = 1000 − W

W = 600 J

Lesson Summary

  • Internal energy is the microscopic energy stored within a system.
  • Heat and work are methods of energy transfer.
  • The First Law of Thermodynamics is a statement of energy conservation.
  • The key equation is ΔU = Q − W.
  • Heat added increases internal energy.
  • Work done by a system decreases internal energy.
  • Thermodynamic principles explain engines, refrigerators, power plants, and biological systems.
  • Energy is never created or destroyed—only transformed or transferred.
