Chapter 8 Summary — Thermal Physics and Thermodynamics

Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we studied the relationship between temperature, heat, energy transfer, thermal expansion, and thermodynamics. We explored how particle motion explains temperature, how heat flows between objects, how materials expand when heated, and how energy conservation governs thermodynamic systems.

Big Idea: Thermal physics explains how energy moves and transforms through heat, temperature changes, expansion, and thermodynamic processes.

1. Temperature

Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of the particles within a substance.

Higher temperature means particles move faster on average.
Temperature Particle Motion
High Faster
Low Slower

2. Heat

Heat is energy transferred from a higher-temperature object to a lower-temperature object.

Heat is energy in transit, while temperature measures the average energy of particles.
Temperature Heat
Measures average particle energy Energy transferred due to temperature difference
Measured in °C, °F, K Measured in Joules (J)

3. Thermal Equilibrium

Thermal equilibrium occurs when two objects reach the same temperature and no net heat transfer occurs between them.

Heat transfer stops when thermal equilibrium is reached.

4. Temperature Scales

Scale Freezing Point of Water Boiling Point of Water
Celsius 0°C 100°C
Fahrenheit 32°F 212°F
Kelvin 273.15 K 373.15 K

Important Conversions

K = °C + 273.15
°C = K - 273.15
°F = (9/5)(°C) + 32
°C = (5/9)(°F - 32)

5. Methods of Heat Transfer

Heat can be transferred in three primary ways.

Method Description Example
Conduction Transfer through direct contact Metal spoon in hot soup
Convection Transfer through fluid motion Boiling water
Radiation Transfer through electromagnetic waves Heat from the Sun
Radiation is the only method that does not require a medium.

6. Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity measures the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of one kilogram of a substance by one degree Celsius.

Materials with larger specific heat capacities require more energy to change temperature.
Material Specific Heat Capacity (J/kg·°C)
Water 4186
Aluminum 900
Iron 450
Copper 385

7. Heat Energy Calculations

Q = mcΔT
Where:
  • Q = Heat Energy (J)
  • m = Mass (kg)
  • c = Specific Heat Capacity
  • ΔT = Temperature Change
This equation determines the amount of heat required to change a substance's temperature.

8. Calorimetry and Energy Conservation

When heat is exchanged between objects in an isolated system:

Heat Lost = Heat Gained
Energy is conserved during heat transfer.

9. Thermal Expansion

Most materials expand when heated because their particles move farther apart.

Heating causes expansion; cooling causes contraction.

Linear Expansion Equation

ΔL = αL₀ΔT
Where:
  • ΔL = Change in Length
  • α = Coefficient of Expansion
  • L₀ = Original Length
  • ΔT = Temperature Change

10. Applications of Thermal Expansion

  • Expansion joints on bridges
  • Railroad track gaps
  • Power lines sagging during hot weather
  • Thermometers
  • Metal lids loosening when heated

11. Internal Energy

Internal energy is the total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of the particles within a system.

Internal energy changes when heat is transferred or work is done.

12. First Law of Thermodynamics

The First Law of Thermodynamics is the law of conservation of energy applied to thermodynamic systems.

ΔU = Q − W
Where:
  • ΔU = Change in Internal Energy
  • Q = Heat Added to the System
  • W = Work Done by the System
Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred or transformed.

13. Understanding the First Law

Situation Result
Heat Added Internal Energy Increases
System Does Work Internal Energy Decreases
Heat Added > Work Done Internal Energy Increases
Work Done > Heat Added Internal Energy Decreases

14. Applications of Thermodynamics

  • Car engines
  • Steam turbines
  • Power plants
  • Refrigerators
  • Air conditioners
  • Heat pumps
  • Human metabolism
Thermodynamics explains how energy is transformed into useful work.

Chapter Review Checkpoint

1. What is the difference between heat and temperature?

Temperature measures average particle kinetic energy, while heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference.

2. Which heat transfer method does not require a medium?

Radiation.

3. Why do railroad tracks have expansion gaps?

To allow thermal expansion during hot weather and prevent buckling.

4. What does Q = mcΔT calculate?

The amount of heat required for a temperature change.

5. State the First Law of Thermodynamics.

ΔU = Q − W
